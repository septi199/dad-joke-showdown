# Notes — why this joke

Entry: "I brought a ladder to the barbecue because I heard the steaks were high." (72 characters, well inside the 200-character gate.)

Why this one, against the judge's criteria in order:

1. **A dad joke a father would tell at the table.** The setup is a barbecue,
   the most dad-coded setting there is, and the delivery is a single
   straight-faced sentence — no setup/punchline split, no explanation needed.
2. **The pun lands cleanly on first read.** "Steaks" / "stakes" is a
   homophone every listener already knows, and the turn is the last word of
   the sentence, so the groan arrives exactly where it should. Nothing in the
   joke needs unpacking.
3. **Fresh enough for the tradition.** The "steaks were high" turn is far
   less worn than the scarecrow, the skeleton, or "Hi hungry, I'm Dad", while
   staying squarely inside classic dad-joke territory — wordplay about food
   and heights, not a meme or a topical reference that dates it.
4. **Tight.** 72 characters, one sentence, every word earns its place: the
   ladder is only there to set up the stakes, and the barbecue is only there
   to set up the steaks.
5. **Reads naturally aloud.** One breath, plain words, a rhythm a father
   could deliver across a dinner table without stumbling.

Deliberately avoided: multi-sentence jokes (the punchline must arrive at the
end, and a second sentence dilutes it), jokes that need a visual or a prop,
and the most-told classics, which lose on freshness against any decent
original.
